PHILOSOPHY OF CARE

We do not begin care
with the symptom alone.

We first review when it began, how it has changed,
and what other concerns appeared with it.

We begin with the full clinical picture,
not the symptom alone.

OUR APPROACH

Even when the concern is similar,
what matters most may be different.

We review when it began, how it has progressed, associated concerns, current medications,
and changes in sleep, digestion, and activity before explaining your current condition.

01 HISTORY & CONTEXT

We begin with
when the concern first appeared.

We ask when you first noticed it, how often it returns,
and what makes it better or worse.

When relevant, we also review associated concerns, medical history, current medications,
and changes in sleep, digestion, and activity.

02 CLINICAL JUDGMENT

We explain which findings
matter most right now.

We bring together the history and examination findings
to identify what should be considered first.

We explain whether further assessment may be needed,
which care options may be considered, and why.

03 CARE PLAN

We build a care plan
you can follow in daily life.

Considering your current condition, goals, and circumstances,
we decide what to address first and what to monitor.

We also explain when progress should be reviewed and what may prompt an adjustment,
so care does not continue without a clear purpose.

WHAT WE VALUE

What we do not overlook during care

01

We distinguish past history from current changes.

We separate long-standing concerns from recent changes
and organize the information relevant to care today.

02

We explain the basis for our judgment.

We explain what we considered important
and whether further assessment may be needed.

03

We set options and priorities together.

Rather than listing every possible method,
we distinguish what should be considered first.

04

We review progress and adjust the plan.

We do not remain fixed on the first plan;
we reassess the next step as your condition changes.

If you are unsure where to begin
with a recurring concern.

You do not need to arrive with a diagnosis or a perfect description.
We can begin with when it started and what has changed.
