WHAT WE OBSERVE

Six things we look at
when planning your care

01

Appetite

Why do you get so hungry in the evenings?

We look at how far apart your meals are, when hunger builds, and what tends to trigger late-night snacking or binge eating. Appetite isn’t just a matter of willpower, so we look at it in the context of your meals across the day and your daily routine.

02

Sleep

Still tired after a full night’s sleep?

We ask about more than how many hours you sleep: how long it takes to fall asleep, whether you wake in the night, how rested you feel in the morning, and how you feel during the day. If needed, we also refer to autonomic nervous system measurements to see how well you are sleeping and recovering.

03

Digestion

Some days, do you feel bloated after eating just a little?

We look at when bloating or discomfort shows up after meals, and which foods or portion sizes keep setting it off. Rather than looking at appetite and how much you eat in isolation, we check them alongside your digestion and eating patterns.

04

Swelling

Does the swelling come and go between morning and evening?

We ask when your face, hands or feet swell, how often it happens, and what you notice day to day. The number on the scale alone doesn’t tell us where you are. If needed, we also refer to body composition and body water measurements. No single reading tells us what is causing the swelling.

05

Bowel habits

Do your bowel habits change a lot?

We look at more than how often you go: what has changed from your usual pattern, any discomfort, and changes before and after meals. We look at bowel habits together with your meals, sleep and daily routine, not on their own.

06

Daily rhythm

When bedtime and meals get pushed back, does your body feel off too?

Your body remembers habits more than it follows the clock. When late nights, skipped meals, big evening meals and days spent sitting still add up, appetite and digestion get thrown off and tiredness builds. Rather than simply telling you to “keep a regular schedule,” we find the point in your real day where things slip first, and one place to start rebuilding.

BE DEER weight care as described in reviews

If this sounds familiar,
consider a BE DEER consultation.

The changes people describe in their reviews didn’t happen to a special few.
They often begin in ordinary lives where meals, sleep and routine have gone off track.

01

I’ve eaten less for three days and the scale hasn’t budged.

Short-term weight depends not only on body fat but on body water, glycogen and bowel movements. We look at your recent weight trend, body composition, what you actually eat and how active you are to tell whether it’s a temporary fluctuation or a real plateau.

02

I thought my diet had worked, then I yo-yoed and gained even more.

After weight loss, the body can show a physiological compensatory response that burns less energy and increases appetite. We look at how fast and by what method you lost weight before, along with changes in muscle mass and eating patterns, to pin down what drove the regain and to plan for keeping the weight off.

03

I tell myself to resist, and next thing I know I’ve opened a delivery app.

Repeated late-night eating and snacking can’t be explained by willpower alone. We look at long gaps between meals, late meals, lack of sleep, stress and appetite changes across your menstrual cycle to find when hunger builds and what keeps setting it off.

04

I often feel puffy, and my body always feels heavy.

Gaining body fat and shifting body fluid can show up as the same change on the scale, but they are different problems. We look at where and when the swelling appears, your digestion and bowel habits, and body composition and body water results, so we can assess weight change and any related discomfort separately.

05

I’m too busy to exercise and too worn out to diet.

The energy you burn each day comes not only from exercise but also from non-exercise activity such as commuting, work and housework. We look at your sleep and recovery, daily activity and eating environment to decide what care you need right now and what comes first.

06

This time, I want to think about my long-term health, not just my weight.

Weight or BMI alone is not enough to assess metabolic risk. We consider abdominal fat, body composition, blood pressure, blood sugar and lipid results, family history and current medications together to set weight-loss goals and decide which metabolic risks to assess first.
