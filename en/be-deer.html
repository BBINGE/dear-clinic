WHAT WE OBSERVE

Six things we look at
when setting the direction of care

01

Appetite

Why does appetite surge only in the evening?

We look at the intervals between meals, the hours when hunger grows, and the situations in which late-night or binge eating repeats. Appetite is not treated as a matter of willpower alone, but read within the flow of the day and your daily rhythm.

02

Sleep

Still tired even after enough sleep?

We ask not only about total sleep hours, but about how long it takes to fall asleep, waking during the night, how rested you feel on waking, and how you hold up through the day. Where useful, measurements related to autonomic function are referred to alongside.

03

Digestion

Are there days when even a little food leaves you bloated?

We look at when fullness or discomfort appears after eating, and with which foods or portions it repeats. Appetite and intake are not read on their own, but together with digestion and meal patterns.

04

Swelling

Does the swelling feel different in the morning and the evening?

We look at when the face, hands or feet swell, how it repeats, and what you notice in daily life. The number on the scale alone does not decide the picture. Where useful, body composition and body water measurements are referred to alongside. No single measurement decides the cause of swelling.

05

Bowel habits

Does your bathroom rhythm change often?

We look at more than frequency: what has changed from your usual pattern, any discomfort, and shifts around meals. Bowel habits are read together with meals, sleep and daily rhythm rather than in isolation.

06

Daily rhythm

When bedtime and meals slip, does the body slip with them?

The body remembers repetition more than the clock. When late nights, skipped meals, heavy evenings and days without movement continue, appetite and digestion lose their footing and fatigue builds. Rather than saying “keep a regular schedule,” we find the hour of your actual day that gives way first, and one point where it can be rebuilt.

BE DEER weight care as described in reviews

If this sounds familiar,
consider a BE DEER consultation.

The changes described in reviews are not the story of a special few.
They often begin in an ordinary life where meals, sleep and rhythm have lost their footing.

01

I eat less for three days and the scale does not move.

Short-term weight is affected not only by body fat but by body water, glycogen and bowel status. We look at recent weight trends, body composition, actual intake and activity together to tell a temporary fluctuation from a genuine plateau.

02

I thought I had succeeded, then gained back even more.

After weight loss, a physiological compensatory response that lowers energy expenditure and raises appetite can appear. We look at how quickly and by what means weight was lost before, along with changes in muscle mass and eating patterns, to separate the factors behind regain and to plan for maintenance.

03

I tell myself to hold out, then find the delivery app open.

Repeated late-night eating and snacking are not explained by willpower alone. We look at long fasting gaps, late meals, short sleep, stress and appetite shifts across the menstrual cycle to find the hours when hunger grows and the conditions under which it repeats.

04

I swell often and my body always feels heavy.

A rise in body fat and a shift in body fluid can look like the same weight change, but they are different problems. We look at where and when the swelling appears, digestion and bowel status, and body composition and body water data, so that weight change and the discomfort alongside it are assessed separately.

05

I am too busy to exercise and too worn out to keep a diet.

Total energy expenditure involves not only exercise but non-exercise activity such as commuting, work and housework. We look at sleep and recovery, everyday activity and your eating environment to set the scope and priorities of care your current state calls for.

06

This time I want to think about my future health, not just weight.

Weight or BMI alone is not enough to assess metabolic risk. Abdominal fat and body composition, blood pressure, glucose and lipid results, family history and current medication are considered together to set weight goals and the priorities of metabolic risk assessment.
